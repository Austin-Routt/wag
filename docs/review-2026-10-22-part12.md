# Review, part 12: drafting tier 1 of the catalogue, and its independent check (2026-10-22)

**What the owner asked:**
1. Draft the tier-1 entries of the scheme catalogue.
2. Have an independent agent check the drafts, and the catalogue, against three sources: the book, the game's NASCQ, and every `.yml` file of `walton-argumentation-schemes-for-carneades-4-master`.
3. Start planning for complete coverage and alignment.

During the work the owner decided six changes to `walton.yml` (§3).

**The results:**
- 32 entries drafted;
- three independent checks, whose 117 findings are applied (§4);
- six source changes;
- a plan for the rest, in [`catalogue-alignment.md`](catalogue-alignment.md).

## 1. How the drafting was done

1. **A guide.** [`wag/data/catalogue/DRAFTING.md`](../wag/data/catalogue/DRAFTING.md) says what to write in every work field. It covers:
   - the answer's shape, with the burden of proof taken from the game's rules;
   - how to code an example and a near miss;
   - the discrepancy kinds;
   - Walton and Macagno's tree, as a fixed vocabulary of groups;
   - the dialogue types.
2. **Two exemplars** drafted by hand: `expert_opinion` and `cause_to_effect`.
3. **A source pack per scheme.** Each was built outside the repository, because it holds the book's text. A pack contains:
   - the compendium entry;
   - the cleaned and upstream `walton.yml` versions, and every category-file version;
   - the English readings;
   - the NASCQ data;
   - the Kialo gold instances, with the claims' texts;
   - the book's ch. 1–3 versions of the scheme, with page numbers.
4. **Four drafting agents,** one per group of related schemes. Each worked in its own copy of the catalogue and wrote its work as patches. The patches were applied to the repository, the catalogue was regenerated, and every merged entry proved byte-identical to the agent's copy.

## 3. The owner's decisions on `walton.yml`

All were made through the cleaning's pipeline, so that `check/run.sh` still rebuilds the file from the upstream original. They are recorded in [`walton-clean/CHANGELOG.md`](scheme-registry/walton-clean/CHANGELOG.md#changes-decided-by-the-owner), "Changes decided by the owner".

1. **33.3 brought in** as `negative_consequences_reasoning`, from the upstream second block. Its variables are filled in and its templates are in our own words.
2. **Two conclusions that met nothing now conclude `¬should_be_performed(A)`:**
   - the new 33.3 scheme, which had threat's `not_bring_about(A)`;
   - the slippery slope's base case, which had `negative_consequences(A)`, used by no other scheme.
3. **`negative_value`** gets its own conclusion, `reason_retracting_goal(V,G)`. Its `negative_affects/3` template is corrected: both had been copied from `positive_value` and said the opposite of the book.
4. **The three consequence schemes get the exception `other_consequences(A)`** for the book's question about opposite consequences (33/CQ3).
   - The prudential scheme already had it.
   - Its template is rewritten to say that the opposite consequences outweigh the cited ones. An exception that holds defeats the argument whatever the weights, so the template has to say so.
5. **The slippery slope's source year** becomes 1992, as the compendium cites it.
   - The book's ch. 4 (p. 114) cites the same p. 93 as "Walton, 1999". That may be another work.
   - Booksellers' listings date the Vale Press edition 1992.
   - A verifier with the book's reference list should settle it.

**What followed from these decisions:**
- `links.json` and `families.json` follow (33.3 exact, 19.2 exact; 96 exact links and 15 partial).
- The batch-2 gold gains the new exception in its 16 consequence instances, so it still validates.
- The annotation engine's copies follow.

**A cleaning typo was fixed** at the same time, outside the owner's decisions: a soft hyphen in the value schemes' citation.

## 4. The independent check

**Who checked.** Three model agents, none of which had drafted. Each was given a brief (kept outside the repository with the packs) and a set of entries:
- the 15 entries of the causal, analogy, source and rule groups, with the two exemplars;
- the 8 consequence and value entries;
- the 9 practical-reasoning entries.

**What they checked.** Every source field and generated item as well as the drafts, against:
- the compendium's transcription;
- the OCR of pp. 1–138;
- the book catalogue;
- the upstream master and every category file;
- the cleaned `walton.yml` and its CHANGELOG;
- the NASCQ, the game's rules and the edge report;
- *The New Dialectic*, page by page;
- the Kialo gold.

**What they found:** 117 findings.

| Severity | Count | Applied |
|---|---|---|
| Error | 4 | 4 |
| Warning | 32 | 32 |
| Note | 81 | 22 record only what was checked and found right. Of the other 59, 58 are applied; one is declined (below). |

The commit that applied them says that 36 notes only record checks; the right figure is 22.

**Kinds of fault found.**
- **Examples that taught the wrong boundary.**
  - Witness testimony's second example reported a routine practice: that is position to know (p. 88).
  - Popular opinion's first example polled the locals: that is position to know ad populum.
- **An example that is not a claim.** One example cited a Kialo claim that `items.json` holds only cut off.
- **Burdens on the wrong side.**
  - from_example's 6.1/CQ1 put the burden for a conclusion target on the proponent.
  - Popular opinion's CQ1 differs from the book's ch. 4 (p. 124). That difference is now recorded.
- **Claims about the game that the NASCQ does not support.** No backup edge lets negative consequences back `CQ:side_effect`.
- **Specialisations the book does not support.**
  - The practical analogies are analogy's counterparts (p. 74), not its subtypes.
  - Established rule is a sibling of rule under 55, not a narrowing of it.
- **Contrasts that disagreed between entries.**
  - Two different thresholds separated a slope from plain consequences.
  - Gradualism was misdescribed.
  - The batch-2 rule was cited under three different labels.
- **Paraphrases that lightly reworded the book's sentence.** They passed the copy check, but break the guide's first rule.
- **Smaller facts.**
  - A stale remark about a template.
  - A second citation of the slope's year.
  - Witness testimony's year (2008 against the book's 2007).
  - Cause to effect's missing source.

**The finding declined.** The suggestion to mark established rule's link to 55.1 partial rather than exact. The link comes from `links.json`, which the catalogue regenerates. The entry's "encoding" discrepancy already records what the formalization leaves out.

**What the checks confirmed.**
- Every formula equals the upstream master's and the category files', except for the cleaning's recorded edits and today's decisions.
- Every book entry, title, page and printed question label is right.
- Every page cited from the book and from ND that the checkers looked up is right.
- The drafters' corrections of generated items are right. These include practical reasoning's swapped question targets, and the moves of the game's questions onto the book questions they ask.

**The check of `cause_to_effect_modal`.** The entry was added after the owner decided N8 later the same day (the alignment plan's C4). One more agent, which had not drafted it, checked it with the same brief, together with the scheme, its links and family, the entries revised for it and the batch-2 guidelines' note. It found 28 things: no errors, 16 warnings, 12 notes, of which 6 record only what it checked and found right.
- **Applied:**
  - the book's weak reading weakens the causal generalization too, which the scheme does not: recorded as open;
  - the steps conclude `may_occur`, not `brings_about(A,G)`, so they explain a consequence but cannot back a consequences argument. The contrasts and decisions that said they do were reworded, and the question is recorded as open;
  - the entry's question answers, summary and one coding read the causal link three ways; they now read it one way;
  - older contrasts in five entries still sent the steps of a proposed measure to `cause_to_effect`;
  - the template `may_occur/1` was a light rewording of the book's minor premise; it is rewritten;
  - `cause_to_effect` gains a forward example of our own, and records that `will_occur` is used for present states and that its steps do not chain;
  - its 13292.2 coding marked an explicit cause as supplied, which `validate.py`'s R4 would flag;
  - the guidelines' note did not say which batch it meant, or that R4 caps the confidence.
- **Applied differently:** the two links of 28 disagreed, partial and exact. `cause_to_effect` stays exact, as both forms of 41 do. The new scheme stays partial, now because its causal premise is not weakened. The partial-link detail, which said `walton.yml` formalizes 28 only in part, was corrected in the entry rather than in the generator. The book quotations in the link's reason and the CHANGELOG were replaced with paraphrases.
- **What it could not check:** the book's ch. 5 on causal argument (p. 168) and Walton 1995, pp. 140–141, the source for 28.

**What none of them could check:**
- the book's chapters 4–8 (pp. 139–307), except pp. 135–8;
- ch. 10 and ch. 11, where the defeasible modus ponens form is treated (pp. 364–6);
- the book's reference list;
