## 2. Is the protocol well defined?

The mechanics are sound; the game is not yet defined. In order of importance:

**2.1 There is nothing to win except points.** The paper defines a game over a disputed claim S₀ with a proponent, an opponent and a proof standard. The code has none of these: no central claim, no sides, no "proof standard met" termination (plan §18, condition 1), and PE is hard-coded. A game ends after N rounds or when everyone abstains, and the higher score wins. No statement's final label matters to the outcome.

**2.2 You cannot defend.** A support move creates a new argument whose conclusion is fixed by the scheme template. It cannot target an existing statement. Once `possible(carbon_tax)` is undermined, no move can back it:
- expert opinion with S = "possible(carbon_tax)" concludes the atom `possible_carbon_tax`, a different statement;
- answering a critical question in the target's favour (the `positive_answer_type: support` side of the mapping) was accepted but silently ignored, and is now rejected as not implemented.

The only defence is a counter-attack on the attacking argument, and only if its scheme has critical questions. The 2026-10-12 fix's statement that "the presumer can reinstate the premise by backing it" holds only for its test, which calls `AddArgumentDirect` and bypasses the game.

**2.3 Four of seven schemes cannot be challenged.** No critical question is asked of expert opinion, position to know, analogy or values. The three thesis-independent ones also back every question, so an attack made with them is permanent. With greedy agents, every one of 400 moves in 20 games is `support/AS:expert_opinion`, and every game is a 70–70 tie. The new linter reports this as its only four warnings.

**2.4 The last mover wins.** Fixing the data alone does not fix the game. In a what-if where every question is asked of every scheme, greedy vs greedy becomes an undercutter chain: 380 of 400 moves, with **the second seat winning 20 of 20**. The causes compound:
- Carneades does not weigh undercutters: any undercutter with weight > 0 defeats its target.
- Each successful undercut earns a flat +15.
- The game is cut off after N rounds, and the second player always moves last.

Two copies of the exploit strategy in the shipped game show the same thing: the second seat wins 10 of 10, 220 to 70.

**2.5 Scoring is local and final.** Points are awarded once, from the labels right after the move, and never revised. An argument that is later defeated keeps its points, and the move that defeats it earns its own. Re-undercutting an argument that is already Out still scores 7 per move. (Self-attacks and exact repeats were the worst cases; both are fixed, section 6.1.) In acyclic play with presumptions, statements are almost never Undecided, so `BasePointsUndecided` rarely applies.

**2.6 The proof standards and weights don't fit.** Base weights are 0.55–0.70. CCE needs a margin above α = 0.5, and BRD additionally needs the loser below β = 0.3. Under CCE or BRD, every contested issue therefore ends with both positions Out. Under PE, equal weights do the same: an equal-weight rebuttal destroys the claim and scores its author nothing.

**2.7 Content is unchecked, and identity is lexical.** In Trusted mode every premise is presumed In at weight 0.01, so any assertion holds until someone attacks it. Bindings are free text, reduced to atoms (`"Carbon-Tax"` = `"carbon tax"`, `"€5"` = `"$5"`). Statements connect across schemes only through identical terms. Practical reasoning concludes `should_be_performed(A)`, while expert opinion concludes a free-text atom, so expert opinion can never support a practical-reasoning conclusion. The plan's binding-reuse dropdowns are not implemented.

**2.8 The critical-question mappings need a domain review.** For example, "Are there other means?" is mapped to a rebuttal of "A should be performed", but an alternative means does not imply that A should not be done. The polarity fields are also inconsistent across the data: `CQ:other_goals` attacks on its *positive* answer, `CQ:possibility` on its *negative* one. The code now reads the attack type as "the answer type that is not `support`" (`nascq.AttackTypeOf`), but the data should say what it means.

**For AI use specifically.** Before this round, an AI player had to be Go code holding a `*GameState`. There is now a JSON view and a JSON-lines player (section 6.1, item 13), so an LLM or a Python loop can play through `wag-cli --p1 json`. Still missing:
- a written action-schema document for prompts;
- an LLM harness that measures invalid-move rates;
- for Paper 2, a specified mapping from a reasoning chain (context, thoughts, summary) to WAG moves. Nothing in the repository or the documents specifies it.

## 7. Critical notes on the fixes package and the documents

**The six patches.** Their claims check out: determinism, exception roles, presumptions, exact rollback, and the issue-aware cycle check. One overclaim is noted in 2.2. Of the README's "not changed" list:
- lossy `TextToAtom`: partly fixed (non-ASCII);
- dead S3/S4/S8: S3 and S8 are now live, S4 removed;
- schemes without ask edges: reported by the linter, data pending;
- wrong line references: fixed;
- module path: unchanged.

**Plans.md (v2.0) vs the code.**
- D3 (no fork) is superseded.
- §7's YAML snapshots were rightly replaced by checkpoints, because the YAML export drops the Theory.
- §8's quoted atoms were rightly replaced by `TextToAtom`, because the scanner reads quotes as char literals.
- §13's S4 can never fire after S5 by construction; semantic types belong in a data lint, which now exists.
- §14's direct rebuttals and underminings are accepted but never enumerated.
- Appendix A's critical questions for expert opinion, position to know and analogy were never added to the data. That omission is the root of 2.3.

**Paper.md: claims to revise before submission.**
- *"NASCQ completeness: every valid move in a Waltonian persuasion dialogue corresponds to a legal action in WAG."* False for this implementation: there are no defence or proleptic moves, and four schemes have no critical questions. It isn't provable in general either, because the NASCQ is a curated, incomplete graph. Restate it as a design goal.
- *"Monotonic score increase."* Not a property of the current scoring, and explicitly false under the planned delta scoring. Drop it.
- *"GroundedLabelling terminates and produces a unique grounded extension."* True only for well-formed graphs. Defect 1 showed that WAG itself could break well-formedness and get nondeterministic labels. State the invariants (now checked after every move) as preconditions.
- *"The mapping [to Nelson–Narens] is not approximate — it is exact."* It's an analogy. In WAG the players, not the system, do the monitoring and control, and nothing corresponds to confidence judgments or learning. Reviewers will reject "exact".
- *"WAG's argument graph IS the reasoning, not a post-hoc explanation."* An LLM emitting WAG moves can be as unfaithful to its internal computation as chain-of-thought (Turpin et al. applies equally). The defensible claim is that the graph is an auditable justification whose acceptability is computed.
- *"The Moderator is an optional enhancement."* In Trusted mode every premise holds until attacked (2.7), so the verdict means "survived the challenges this opponent chose to raise". For Paper 2's validation use, opponent strength and content judgment are part of the method and must be reported. The Gemini critique of a "moderator bottleneck" is closer to right than the paper allows.
- *"WAG is zero-shot (vs. PRMs)."* The evaluation step is, but authoring the NASCQ and the reasoning-chain-to-CAES mapping is the hard, expert-dependent part. Say so.

**Review.md.** Superseded. Its "fully verified" adapter later turned out to contain the six defects of 2026-10-06, plus defects 1–4 above.

## 8. Validating stringently

**Added in this round**
- `GameState.CheckInvariants`, checked after every move of 120 random three-player games on two NASCQ shapes:
  - graph links;
  - at most one issue per statement;
  - at most one In position per issue;
  - no statement In together with its negation;
  - acyclicity;
  - the applied labels equal three fresh labellings (catches map-order dependence);
  - roles and owners;
  - score accounting.

- `GameState.Simulate` is exact: simulating any enumerated move leaves graph, labels, weights, scores, history and digest unchanged, and the simulated result equals the real one.

- Fuzzing of `TextToAtom`: output always parses as an atom, and ASCII input matches the old function.
- `nascq.Lint` and `game.LintData` (structure, cross-check with the YAML, Language arity), run in tests and by `wag-cli --lint`.

## 9. Recommendations (each needs your decision; section 10)

1. **Thesis, sides and verdict.** A central claim, a proponent and an opponent. The winner is decided by the claim's label at the end under the configured proof standard; points become the tiebreak.
2. **Termination by quiescence.** The game ends after a full round of passes. Max turns stays as a safety cap, reported as "unresolved" rather than as a win. This also removes the cut-off half of the last-mover advantage.
3. **A "back" move.** Let a scheme whose conclusion is a bare variable (expert opinion `[S]`, position to know `[S]`, analogy `[A]`) bind that variable to an existing statement ID instead of to free text. Expert opinion could then back `possible(carbon_tax)` or argue `¬should_be_performed(x)`. This is cheap to add and gives the game defence.
4. **Critical questions for every scheme.** Plan Appendix A already specifies them for expert opinion, position to know and analogy; the linter will enforce coverage.
5. **Undercutter balance.** Either require an undercutter to outweigh its target (or make undercutters issues), or remove the flat +15 and reward only changes to the central issue.
6. **End-state or delta scoring**, which removes the remaining farming.
7. **Calibrate** the base weights against the CCE and BRD thresholds.
8. **A shared vocabulary of entities.** Reuse previous bindings (the plan's dropdowns) and show the player's original text next to atoms.
