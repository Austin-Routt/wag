# Proposal D001, D028, D029: The analogy family in two-case form

Generated by `wag-cli --proposal` from [`D001-D028-D029.yml`](D001-D028-D029.yml); edit that file, not this one. The decisions are in [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md); how proposals work is in [`process.md`](../process.md) §5.

**What changes.** The book's argument from analogy (7) and its practical counterparts (8.1, 8.2) use one letter for what holds in both cases: a claim A in analogy, an action x in the practical forms, made relative to each case. walton.yml's analogy cannot do that: its premise says the target's claim held in the source case, which is false whenever the two differ (D001). Its practical analogies keep the situation in their conclusions, carry_out_right(A2,X), so they are sound, but they meet no other scheme: they neither rebut each other nor support or attack a practical claim (D029). The proposal gives all three one two-case form. A claim (P1, P2) or an action (X1, X2) in each case, joined by a correspondence premise. Similarity, differences and the counter-case between the two cases. Analogy concludes the target claim; the practical analogies conclude that the target action should, or should not, be performed, so that they meet practical reasoning and the consequence arguments about the same action, and each other. **What is ours, not the book's.** The correspondence premise, which makes the book's shared letter explicit. The counter-case exception, which keeps walton.yml's design (a case closer to the target undercuts; one merely similar to the source, the book's CQ3 as printed, is a rebuttal). The practical analogies' three exceptions and questions, for which the book prints none (D028; it treats them as analogy's practical counterparts). The two templates. **Polarity.** The two practical analogies become an exact positive/negative pair: only their base premise (right / wrong, contraries, as the book has them) and the sign of their conclusion (contradictories) differ. Analogy needs no pair: its "(false)" form is a negated claim in the bindings (examples 3, 8). **What to judge in the renderings:** whether every line says something true and sensible; the two new templates; and the practical analogies' conclusion, "action X2 should be performed", in which the situation is carried by the similarity premise and by X2 itself.

### 1. The tobacco tax and a meat tax

**as an argument**, with `analogy`; Kialo 13292.61.

> A tax for tobacco significantly reduced its consumption.

Bindings: P1 = “taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption”; P2 = “taxing meat would significantly reduce meat consumption”; C1 = “a tax on tobacco”; C2 = “a tax on meat”.

- “A tax on tobacco” is similar to “a tax on meat”.
- “Taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption” is true in case “a tax on tobacco”.
- “Taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption”, in the other case, corresponds to “taxing meat would significantly reduce meat consumption” in this one.
- *Unless:* There are relevant differences between case “a tax on tobacco” and case “a tax on meat”.
- *Unless:* There is a case closer to “a tax on meat” than “a tax on tobacco” is, in which the corresponding claim failed.
- **So:** Taxing meat would significantly reduce meat consumption.

Pressed with its questions:

- **7/CQ1**, at the exception “There are relevant differences between case “a tax on tobacco” and case “a tax on meat””: Tobacco is bought by a minority, often and in small amounts; meat is a staple that most households buy every week, and demand for staples changes little with price.
- **7/CQ2**, at the premise ““Taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption” is true in case “a tax on tobacco””: Smoking fell while advertising bans and smoke-free laws came in; was it the tax that cut consumption?
- **7/CQ3**, at the exception “There is a case closer to “a tax on meat” than “a tax on tobacco” is, in which the corresponding claim failed”: Suppose a tax on sugary drinks, a food bought by most households and so a closer case, left consumption almost unchanged.

*Note:* The batch-2 gold codes this claim with the current formula, A bound to the target claim and the source claim kept in a note (audit N12).
