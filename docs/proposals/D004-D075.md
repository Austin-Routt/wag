**What changes.**
- **D005, D004.** practical_reasoning1 and practical_reasoning2 state the
  agent's goal as their first premise, goal_of_agent(P,G), the schemes
  library's predicate; it maps the book's goal premise (22.4's Premise 1,
  22.1's Major Premise), which no formula stated. P is the agent who
  would do A. Both get the conflicting-goal exception scoped to the agent
  and the goal, incompatible_goal(P,G): practical_reasoning1 newly, where
  22.4/CQ1 had no formula, and practical_reasoning2 in place of its
  unscoped incompatible_goal(G). Their conclusion stays
  should_be_performed(A).
- **D088.** The goal's possibility and the agent's are two claims:
  possible(G) becomes possible_goal(G) and possible(A) becomes
  possible_bring_about(P,A), P bound by the goal premise. Neither says
  that the means is available now or sure to work.
- **D089.** The three "another action would do better" exceptions name
  the agent: realize_more_effectively(P,G,A),
  bring_about_more_effectively(P,S2,A) and promote_more_effectively(P,V,A).
  "More effectively" means reaching the goal more surely, sooner or at
  lower cost; a preference for another value is not this claim. The
  situation compared is the current one (Walton and Gordon 2015, p. 515:
  "when performed in S1"), fixed per game or case and checked at the
  record level, not added to the formulas.
- **D070, D091.** The side-effect exception of practical reasoning's
  schemes becomes bad_outweighs(A,G): that A has bad consequences that
  outweigh the goal G. A plain side effect is argued as negative
  consequences, a rebuttal at the argument's conclusion; the claim that
  it outweighs G is the exception, answered by bad_outweighs_good. This
  outweighing reading is WAG's interpretation: the source's side-effect
  exception (Walton and Gordon 2015, p. 515) is any side effect that
  demotes a value. 22.4/CQ7, 22.1/CQ5 and the condition schemes' 22.3/CQ4
  target it.
- **D030, D090.** The necessary- and sufficient-condition schemes state
  their agent P in each premise, as the schemes library's repair does,
  give the set of means its own variable S, and use G for the goal (A was
  the book's letter for it). The goal's possibility, possible_goal(G), is
  apart from the means', possible_bring_about(P,X). WAG declares the
  best-choice reading for both: a strictly better means the agent can
  take defeats the recommendation, realize_more_effectively(P,G,X), and an
  equally good one does not (D006). The sufficient-condition scheme has
  no mere-alternative exception, so it no longer undercuts itself (version
  1's alternative_means(P,A,X) held whenever its own set had a second
  member). The necessary-condition scheme's objection is that the goal
  can be reached without any means of the set,
  goal_achievable_without_any(P,G,S); its answer must name a route the
  agent can take that reaches G and uses no member of S. Defeating the
  recommendation does not show that X would not reach the goal. Their side
  effect becomes bad_outweighs(X,G), still an exception (D003, D091), and
  their conflicting goal incompatible_goal(P,G); the other goal leaves the
  formula, and an answer names it.
- **D091.** practical_inference takes goal_of_agent(P,G),
  possible_bring_about(P,A), incompatible_goal(P,G),
  realize_more_effectively(P,G,A) and bad_outweighs(A,G), as practical
  reasoning has them, and keeps its own objection that the action works
  against its goal, scoped to the agent, incompatible_consequences(P,A,G),
  apart from the outweighing claim.
- **D063.** Practical reasoning's own schemes conclude should_be_performed:
  practical_inference should_be_performed(A) (was carry_out_action(A)), the
  two condition schemes should_be_performed(X) (was bringing_about_action(X)).
  The conclusions stay one-place (D005); who performs an action comes from
  a structured action-to-actor mapping with its aliases, at the record
  level (stage 2's identity contract), not from the action's name.
- **D064 and D075: the first answer schemes.** An answer scheme concludes
  the exception it answers, and its premises bind every variable of it.
  - bad_outweighs_good and good_outweighs_bad, C003's pair: a consequence
    H of A, bad (good), that matters more than the G of an argument of
    the other sign. bad_outweighs_good is also D075's side effect that
    outweighs, since D070's exception is bad_outweighs(A,G).
  - better_means: another action A2 that the agent P can take, which
    would realize P's goal G more effectively than A; it concludes
    realize_more_effectively(P,G,A). Its premises are the answered
    argument's own goal premise, goal_of_agent(P,G), P's ability to take
    A2, possible_bring_about(P,A2) (a premise, not a presumption: what
    nobody raises would otherwise be presumed), that A2 brings about G, and
    the comparison, more_effective_means(P,A2,G,A). A2 must be another
    action than A: the game and the validators check it, since a
    Carneades guard would not act in WAG's play.
  - They have no critical questions of their own. Each premise can be
    challenged, which the game asks instead (game-rules.md §2); version
    1's labels were borrowed from other entries (33/CQ1) or invented
    (C003/Q1, D075/Q1), and the catalogue could not carry them.
- **No change.** D003: the questions stay exceptions, the critic's to
  support; who must answer a question that is asked is the game's
  question protocol (D075). D006: an equally good alternative is no reason
  against A, so no scheme here has an exception for one.

**Readings.** Each follows from a decision's text and its reasons.
1. **One formula for one conflicting goal.** D004 reuses practical_reasoning2's
  predicate "to keep one formula for one idea", scoped to the agent and the
  goal, so every scheme here uses incompatible_goal(P,G). The other goal
  must clash with G itself: a goal set back by the means is a consequence
  of that means, a rebuttal or bad_outweighs (guidelines §3). An answer at
  incompatible_goal(P,G) defeats every means to G, as it should.
2. **The side effect that outweighs is D055's bad_outweighs(A,G).** The
  reason for the action is its goal G, and D055 already formalizes the
  claim that A's bad consequences outweigh G as positive_consequences'
  exception. With one formula, a practical argument and a consequence
  argument for the same A and G share the exception, and one weighing
  defeats both. Two things follow. A decisive side effect must be weighed
  once per goal (D055's limit (1) now applies to practical reasoning). S1
  leaves the exception: the situation is the case's, at the record level
  (D089). side_effects/2 is left as an orphan template.
3. **The condition schemes.** D030 adopts the library's separation "so that
  no variable is left unbound"; D090 adds the best-choice reading, the
  typed objection of the necessary-condition scheme and the goal variable
  G. 22.3's alternatives premise says that each means would be enough, as
  ch. 3 prints the sufficient-condition schema (Walton, Reed and Macagno
  2008, p. 96); the compendium's own 22.3 repeats 22.2's "necessary"
  (p. 323), which links.json records as a misprint. The selection premise
  keeps the book's allowance of an acceptable or the best means; the
  exception is the best-choice reading. alternative_means stays 2-ary,
  for two_person_practical, and is not reused here.
4. **Positive binding, in part a deferral.** The exceptions now name the
  agent, so an answer for another agent no longer concludes the same
  formula. What the formulas cannot do is tie an answer to the answered
  argument. The validators check now that a better means is another action
  than A, that its goal premise is the answered argument's own, and that it
  records its comparison (rule R9). That A2 is P's action, that the
  argument's P is the agent the case deliberates for, and the situation
  compared wait for the identity contract (before the first gold draft) and
  the game (C5). The engine's own inference would derive arguments for
  another agent from presumed premises (the review's graph E2c); WAG's play
  runs no inference, so only these record checks guard against it.
5. **Premise order.** goal_of_agent(P,G) comes first, where the book prints
  the goal premise. Nothing reads premises by position.

**Limits, recorded.**
- Both of two contrary comparisons can be accepted (matters_more(H,G) and
  matters_more(G,H): D053's contraries are not enforced, limit (2) of
  positive_consequences), and a comparison names no one whose ordering it
  is.
- An answer bound to another agent or another goal is refused by the
  record checks (R8, R9), not by the engine. That its other action A2 is
  open to the agent P is its premise possible_bring_about(P,A2), which a
  reply challenges; the identity contract's action-to-actor mapping and
  the game (C5) are to check it.
- One claim that the action works against its own goal defeats practical
  inference, whose exception it is, and leaves practical_reasoning2 for the
  same A, G and P standing (D091 kept the two objections apart).
- D090's answer contract (the answer names a route the agent can take that
  reaches G and uses no member of S) is decided, but the engine accepts any
  argument concluding goal_achievable_without_any(P,G,S); the CQ answer
  contract (stage 2, item F) and the game (C5) enforce it.

### 1. A bike lane on Main Street

**as an argument**, with `practical_reasoning1`; our own text.

> East-side commuters now sit in traffic for an hour each way. A protected bike lane on Main Street would let many of them cycle instead, which would cut congestion, and less congestion means cleaner air for everyone. The council should build the lane.

Bindings: A = “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street”; S1 = “east-side commuters spending an hour in traffic each way”; S2 = “many east-side commuters cycling to work”; G = “less congestion in the city”; V = “public health”; P = “the city council”.

- One of the goals of “the city council” is “less congestion in the city”.
- “East-side commuters spending an hour in traffic each way” is the case in the current circumstances.
- Performing “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street” in “east-side commuters spending an hour in traffic each way” would bring about “many east-side commuters cycling to work”.
- “Less congestion in the city” would be realized in “many east-side commuters cycling to work”.
- Achieving the goal “less congestion in the city” would promote the value “public health”.
- *Presumed:* “Public health” is a legitimate value.
- *Presumed:* “Less congestion in the city” is a worthy goal.
- *Presumed:* It is possible for “the city council” to bring about “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street”.
- *Unless:* There is a way for “the city council” to reach the state “many east-side commuters cycling to work” more effectively than “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street”.
- *Unless:* There is a way for “the city council” to realize the goal “less congestion in the city” more effectively than “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street”.
- *Unless:* There is a way for “the city council” to promote the value “public health” more effectively than “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street”.
- *Unless:* The action “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street” has bad consequences that outweigh “less congestion in the city”.
- *Unless:* Another goal of “the city council” is incompatible with “less congestion in the city”.
- **So:** Action “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street” should be performed.

Pressed with its questions:

- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “One of the goals of “the city council” is “less congestion in the city””: Less congestion is not one of the council's goals; its transport plan is about road safety, and says so.
- **22.4/CQ1**, at the exception “Another goal of “the city council” is incompatible with “less congestion in the city””: The council's plan to draw shoppers' cars back into the centre, to revive its high street, needs more traffic in the city, not less.
- **22.4/CQ3**, at the exception “There is a way for “the city council” to realize the goal “less congestion in the city” more effectively than “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street””: Re-timing the lights on the ring road would cut congestion as much, at a tenth of the cost.
- **22.4/CQ7**, at the exception “The action “the city council building a protected bike lane on Main Street” has bad consequences that outweigh “less congestion in the city””: The lane would take the street's parking and close its shops, and losing them matters more than shorter queues (example 18 argues it).

*Note:* P is the agent of A; the record's action-to-actor mapping says so, not A's name. A shop owner's reply that the lane would hurt trade, with no comparison, would be negative consequences, a rebuttal at the conclusion; 22.4/CQ7 is the claim that the harm outweighs G. The other goal in 22.4/CQ1 clashes with G itself; a goal that only the lane would set back is a consequence of the lane.
