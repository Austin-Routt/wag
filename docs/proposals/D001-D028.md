# Proposal D001, D028: Analogy between two cases, and the practical analogies' difference exception

Generated by `wag-cli --proposal` from [`D001-D028.yml`](D001-D028.yml); edit that file, not this one. The decisions are in [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md); how proposals work is in [`process.md`](../process.md) §5.

**D001.** The book's argument from analogy compares two cases: a claim held in the source case, the cases are alike, so the corresponding claim holds in the target case. walton.yml's `analogy` names only the source case and uses one proposition for both, so its premise says that the target's claim held in the source case, which is false whenever the two claims differ. The owner chose to replace the formula with a two-case form (option C, 2026-10-22): source and target cases (Cs, Ct), source and target claims (Ps, Pt), and a premise that the target claim is the counterpart of the source finding. It reuses walton.yml's predicates for similarity, the source finding and differences, and adds two: `corresponds/2`, and `closer_counter_case/2` for the counter-case. The current exception, `more_on_point(A,C)`, reads a claim as false "in another case", which only works while one claim serves every case; the first rendering of these examples showed it failing. The new exception keeps the current design: a counter-case closer to the target than the source undercuts the argument, and one merely similar to the source (the book's CQ3 as printed) is argued against the conclusion as a rebuttal. **D028.** The two practical analogies (8.1, 8.2), for which the book prints no questions, get analogy's difference exception with both situations as arguments, `relevant_differences(A1,A2)`, so that one game question about differences can serve all three schemes. **What to judge in the renderings:** whether each line says something true and sensible; in particular the two new templates, `corresponds/2` and `closer_counter_case/2`.

### 1. The tobacco tax and a meat tax

**as an argument**, with `analogy`; Kialo 13292.61.

> A tax for tobacco significantly reduced its consumption.

Bindings: Ps = “taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption”; Pt = “taxing meat would significantly reduce meat consumption”; Cs = “a tax on tobacco”; Ct = “a tax on meat”.

- “A tax on tobacco” is similar to “a tax on meat”.
- “Taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption” is true in case “a tax on tobacco”.
- What “taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption” says of the other case, “taxing meat would significantly reduce meat consumption” says of this one.
- *Unless:* There are relevant differences between case “a tax on tobacco” and case “a tax on meat”.
- *Unless:* There is a case closer to “a tax on meat” than “a tax on tobacco” is, in which the corresponding claim failed.
- **So:** Taxing meat would significantly reduce meat consumption.

Pressed with its questions:

- **7/CQ1**, at the exception “There are relevant differences between case “a tax on tobacco” and case “a tax on meat””: Tobacco is bought by a minority, often and in small amounts; meat is a staple that most households buy every week, and demand for staples changes little with price.
- **7/CQ2**, at the premise ““Taxing tobacco significantly reduced tobacco consumption” is true in case “a tax on tobacco””: Smoking fell while advertising bans and smoke-free laws came in; was it the tax that cut consumption?
- **7/CQ3**, at the exception “There is a case closer to “a tax on meat” than “a tax on tobacco” is, in which the corresponding claim failed”: Suppose a tax on sugary drinks, a food bought by most households and so a closer case, left consumption almost unchanged.

*Note:* The batch-2 gold codes this claim with the current formula, A bound to the target claim and the source claim kept in a note (audit N12).
