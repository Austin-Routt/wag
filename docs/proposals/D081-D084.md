The "Now" columns below show `walton.yml` as it is since, so they equal the proposed ones, but for a scheme a later decision has changed, which is marked; the formulas before the change are in `walton-clean/CHANGELOG.md`, among the owner's decisions.

**What changes.** Three schemes, in two decisions.

- **D084, is/2 becomes is_a/2.** Since 2026-11-22 WAG runs the schemes
  library's engine, which reserves `is`, `true` and `false` as predicate
  names: in a rule body GoCHR runs is/2 as an assignment. group_2 concludes
  is(M,Q) and rule_physical_world_1 has is(W,D) as a premise, so the
  validator fails both schemes and the language key, and the engine's own
  inference never draws group_2's conclusion. Only the name changes: is_a/2
  is the schemes library's name, and the template stays "%s is %s.". WAG
  instantiates schemes with explicit parameters, which works with either
  name.
- **D081, the sorites chain names its property.** The chain premise,
  reapplication_sequence(A2,A), says that a chain of steps leads from the
  clear case A2 to the case A, but not what the steps are about. Every other
  formula of the scheme is about one property P: the clear case has P, a
  neighbour of a case with P has P, and the conclusion is that A may have
  P. With a binary chain, a chain built for one property (wealth: a fortune
  less one coin is still a fortune) satisfies the premise of an argument
  about another (baldness). reapplication_sequence(A2,A,P) ties the chain
  to the property the argument is about. The book's chain premise names no
  property (p. 341); scoping it to P is our reading, which keeps the
  argument about one property throughout, as its other premises and its
  conclusion are.

D081 also approves, unchanged, three repairs the cleaning made on
2026-10-22 that awaited the owner (the CHANGELOG's "Repairs that need the
owner's approval"): moral_justification_ad_populum_excuse concludes that the
speaker's action is morally acceptable, as the book's conclusion says
(p. 312); sacrifice's value is the declared variable V, shared with the
conclusion (p. 322); rhetorical_oppositions_normative keeps WAG's role
order, treatment first, which its templates follow (p. 318). They are
already in walton.yml; this proposal does not change them, so they are
listed under the effects, not as schemes.

**What is ours, not the book's:** the property in the chain premise; the
chain template, in our own words; the name is_a.

**Not part of D084:** the schemes library also adds a premise to
rule_physical_world_1, that W and D are referentially coextensive. That
changes what the scheme asserts (class inclusion, a whale is a mammal, is
not coextension), and would need a decision of its own.

**What to judge in the renderings:** whether the chain premise reads as a
chain of steps about one property; whether the examples' challenges are

## The examples, rendered

Each example binds the scheme's variables to phrases; every formula is then read with `walton.yml`'s templates and the proposal's. Read each line as the game would show it, and ask whether it says something true and sensible.

### 1. An orchestra's discipline

**as an argument**, with `group_2`; our own text.

> The city orchestra is known for its discipline, and Mara has just joined it as oboist, so Mara is disciplined.

Bindings: G = “the city orchestra”; Q = “disciplined”; M = “Mara, its new oboist”.

- Group “the city orchestra” has the quality “disciplined”.
- “Mara, its new oboist” is a member,idea, habit, custom, product or method of “the city orchestra”.
- If “the city orchestra” has “disciplined”, its members ideas, habits, customs, products or methods “Mara, its new oboist” will have it as well.
- **So:** “Mara, its new oboist” is “disciplined”.

Pressed with its questions:

- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “If “the city orchestra” has “disciplined”, its members ideas, habits, customs, products or methods “Mara, its new oboist” will have it as well”: Players join on the strength of an audition, not of their habits; a new member need not yet share what the orchestra is known for.

### 2. A refined cuisine and one of its dishes

**as an argument**, with `group_2`; our own text.

> French cooking is refined, and cassoulet is a French dish, so cassoulet is refined.

Bindings: G = “French cooking”; Q = “refined”; M = “cassoulet”.

- Group “French cooking” has the quality “refined”.
- “Cassoulet” is a member,idea, habit, custom, product or method of “French cooking”.
- If “French cooking” has “refined”, its members ideas, habits, customs, products or methods “cassoulet” will have it as well.

Pressed with its questions:

- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “If “French cooking” has “refined”, its members ideas, habits, customs, products or methods “cassoulet” will have it as well”: The refinement belongs to the restaurant tradition; cassoulet is a farmhouse stew, and a quality of the whole need not be a quality of each part.
- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise ““Cassoulet” is a member,idea, habit, custom, product or method of “French cooking””: Cassoulet is a dish of one region, Languedoc, more than of French cooking as the argument means it.

### 3. From a member to the group

**a near miss** of `group_2`: it is `group_1`; our own text.

Bindings: G = “the city orchestra”; Q = “discipline”; M = “Mara”.

### 4. A vixen and a female fox

**as an argument**, with `rule_physical_world_1`; our own text.

> A vixen is a female fox, so "vixen" means the same as "female fox".

Bindings: W = “a vixen”; D = “a female fox”.

- “A vixen” is “a female fox”.
- Whenever expressions are referentially coextensive, they are synonymous.
- *Unless:* First premise not necessary true.
- **So:** “A vixen” means the same as “a female fox”.

Pressed with its questions:

- **54.1/Rebuttal Factor 1**, at the exception “First premise not necessary true”: Is it necessary that a vixen is a female fox, or only how things happen to be? (Here it holds by the meaning of the words, so the rebuttal fails.)

### 5. A whale and a mammal

**as an argument**, with `rule_physical_world_1`; our own text.

> A whale is a mammal, so "whale" means the same as "mammal".

Bindings: W = “a whale”; D = “a mammal”.

- “A whale” is “a mammal”.
- Whenever expressions are referentially coextensive, they are synonymous.
- *Unless:* First premise not necessary true.
- **So:** “A whale” means the same as “a mammal”.

Pressed with its questions:

- **54.1/Rebuttal Factor 1**, at the exception “First premise not necessary true”: That whales are mammals was found by zoology; it is not a necessary truth, so the warrant does not apply.
- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “Whenever expressions are referentially coextensive, they are synonymous”: Expressions can pick out the same things without meaning the same; "creature with a heart" and "creature with kidneys" are the classic case.

*Note:* Class inclusion is not coextension, which is why the schemes library adds a coextension premise; that is not part of D084.

### 6. A whale classified by a definition

**a near miss** of `rule_physical_world_1`: it is `definition_to_verbal_classification`; our own text.

> A whale breathes air and suckles its young, which is the definition of a mammal, so a whale is a mammal.

Bindings: O = “a whale”; G = “mammal”; D = “breathing air and suckling the young”.

- “A whale” satisfies definition “breathing air and suckling the young”.
- Objects which satisfy definition “breathing air and suckling the young” are classified as instances of class “mammal”.
- *Unless:* “Breathing air and suckling the young” is an inadequate definition of “mammal”.
- **So:** “A whale” is an instance of class “mammal”.

*Note:* It concludes that the thing belongs to a class, not that two words mean the same; argument from a definition to a verbal classification.

### 7. The bald man

**as an argument**, with `sorites_slippery_slope`; our own text.

> A man with no hair at all is bald, and one hair more makes no man less bald, so a man with twenty thousand hairs may be bald, for all anyone can prove.

Bindings: A = “a man with twenty thousand hairs”; P = “baldness”; A1 = “a man with one hair”; A2 = “a man with no hair at all”.

- It is clearly beyond contention that “a man with no hair at all” , has “baldness”.
- If “a man with no hair at all”, has “baldness”, then “a man with one hair” has it too.
- A chain of modus ponens steps leads from “a man with no hair at all”, in the clear area, through the grey area to “a man with twenty thousand hairs”, each step concerning “baldness”.
- **So:** “A man with twenty thousand hairs”, may have “baldness”, for all we know or can prove.

Pressed with its questions:

- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “A chain of modus ponens steps leads from “a man with no hair at all”, in the clear area, through the grey area to “a man with twenty thousand hairs”, each step concerning “baldness””: Each added hair seems harmless, but the steps add up; run twenty thousand times, the chain passes men with a full head of hair, so not every step holds.
- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “A chain of modus ponens steps leads from “a man with no hair at all”, in the clear area, through the grey area to “a man with twenty thousand hairs”, each step concerning “baldness””: The steps count hairs, but whether a man looks bald depends on how the hair is spread, so the chain does not stay about baldness.

*Note:* The inductive premise is coded as its first step, from no hair to one, which is true; what a reader presses is the chain that repeats it. The chain is about baldness. A chain about wealth (a fortune less one coin is still a fortune) does not satisfy this premise, because its third argument is wealth; with the old binary premise it did.

### 8. The heap

**as an argument**, with `sorites_slippery_slope`; our own text.

> A million grains of sand make a heap, and taking one grain from a heap leaves a heap, so a single grain may be a heap, for all anyone can prove.

Bindings: A = “a single grain”; P = “the property of being a heap”; A1 = “a million grains less one”; A2 = “a million grains of sand”.

- It is clearly beyond contention that “a million grains of sand” , has “the property of being a heap”.
- If “a million grains of sand”, has “the property of being a heap”, then “a million grains less one” has it too.
- A chain of modus ponens steps leads from “a million grains of sand”, in the clear area, through the grey area to “a single grain”, each step concerning “the property of being a heap”.
- **So:** “A single grain”, may have “the property of being a heap”, for all we know or can prove.

Pressed with its questions:

- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “A chain of modus ponens steps leads from “a million grains of sand”, in the clear area, through the grey area to “a single grain”, each step concerning “the property of being a heap””: Long before the last grain the pile stops being anything anyone would call a heap; the chain cannot run all the way down.
- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise “It is clearly beyond contention that “a million grains of sand” , has “the property of being a heap””: Whether a million grains spread thin over a beach make a heap is itself in doubt; the clear case has to be stated more carefully.

### 9. Retaking one exam

**a near miss** of `sorites_slippery_slope`: it is `full_slippery_slope`; our own text.

> If students may retake one exam, they will ask to retake every exam, and then the grades will mean nothing.

Bindings: C0 = “letting students retake one exam”; C1 = “letting students retake every exam”; C2 = “grades meaning nothing”.

- At the outset, “letting students retake one exam” is presumed acceptable.
- In the chain of cases from “letting students retake one exam” through “letting students retake every exam” to “grades meaning nothing”, every case brings on the following one, through causal, precedent or analogical links.
- Opinion runs so that whoever comes to accept one case in the series from “letting students retake one exam” to “grades meaning nothing” will be carried on to accept the next.
- *Presumed:* Accepting a case in the series from “letting students retake one exam” to “grades meaning nothing” will in fact carry people on to the next one.
- **So:** “Letting students retake one exam” cannot be accepted after all, whatever the initial presumption.

*Note:* Each step brings on the next through precedent and pressure, not through the vagueness of a word; the full slippery slope, not the sorites.
