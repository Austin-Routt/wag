**What changes.** One scheme is added, finding_out_first, WAG's answer
scheme for the exception worth_finding_out_first(A,G) of the two bridges
of possible consequences (bridge_from_possible_good and
bridge_from_possible_harm, D008). It concludes the exception from three
premises, the conditions of the owner's declaration under D053:
- settles_whether(I,A,G): an investigation I would settle whether A brings
  about G, reliably enough for the decision on A (the declaration's
  "sufficiently ... reliable to resolve the relevant question");
- done_in_time(I,A): I can be done before the decision on A must be taken
  ("sufficiently timely");
- worth_its_costs(I,A,G): the costs and risks of I are worth bearing to
  know whether A brings about G before that possibility counts in the
  decision ("its costs and risks must justify obtaining the information
  first").
The investigation I is a variable of its own, so that an answer names the
inquiry it relies on (a trial, a study, a check of the record), and each
condition can be challenged on its own. The first two premises keep apart
what the declaration keeps apart: done_in_time is the deadline, whether I
ends before the decision on A must be taken; worth_its_costs is what I
costs and risks (money, effort, a harm the investigation itself may do), so
that a test that comes too late is challenged at done_in_time and a test
whose cost or risk is too great at worth_its_costs.

**The declaration** (the owner, 2026-11-25, recorded under D053), whole:
"I declare `worth_finding_out_first(A,G)` neutral as to polarity between
`bridge_from_possible_good` and `bridge_from_possible_harm`, when both
concern the same action A, consequence G, and decision context. The
exception means that it is worthwhile to determine whether A would bring
about G before allowing that possibility to influence the decision. The
investigation must be sufficiently timely and reliable to resolve the
relevant question, and its costs and risks must justify obtaining the
information first. The critic bears the burden of establishing the
exception. Merely asserting that the consequence could be investigated,
without establishing the stronger conditions, does not satisfy that
burden. An unresolved tie goes against the critic under the declared
evaluation rule. When established, the exception defeats both applicable
modal bridges, regardless of which side supports it. It does not defeat
the modal consequence arguments themselves, independent categorical
arguments, bridges concerning different consequences, or arguments in
other decision contexts. It does not suspend the decision on A; remaining
arguments continue to determine the outcome under the game's rules. This
is a deliberate WAG formalization consistent with D003, D008, D053 and the
revised C3 proposal."

**Why.** D095 (3) decided it: no scheme concluded the exception, so the game
could not tell a well-made answer from a bare claim of knowability, both
made with Other. With this scheme the declaration's "stronger conditions"
are premises its arguer supports, each open to challenge, and the game
refuses Other at the question (D095 (3), item F of c5-game.md). None of
the premises says that the decision on A waits: the exception leaves the
possibility out of the decision until it is found out, not the decision
(the declaration; D008; guidelines OC15). The template of
worth_finding_out_first/2 already reads the three conditions (D008-D074);
the scheme makes them its premises. The decision context the declaration
names is not a formula: the case record's contexts (R15) and the game's
profile carry it.

**Its sources.** The exception is WAG's formalization (D008, D053), not the
book's. Its conditions follow what Walton says of a shift to
information-seeking inside a critical discussion, made to improve its
quality (Walton 1998, The New Dialectic, p. 203); of delaying a decision to
collect information, often the most prudent course, though delaying may
itself be a course of action with consequences; and of choosing sources
one has reason to think reliable (Walton 2006, Fundamentals of Critical
Argumentation, p. 85; also Walton 2004, Relevance in Argumentation, p. 14,
on a failure to act). That a reply to a question is not always an answer
to it (Walton 1989, Question-Reply Argumentation, p. 6), and that who
raises an exception must produce some evidence for it, not only ask
(Walton 2014, Burden of Proof, Presumption and Argumentation, p. 22), are
the reasons a bare claim of knowability is not an answer. In Walton's
account of arguments from ignorance, the defensive side claims that
further investigation is not necessary (Walton 1996, Arguments from
Ignorance, p. 122); this scheme argues the opposite of one such claim: that
a particular investigation is worth making before a possibility counts.

**What it does not change.** The exception's scope and burden stay the
declaration's: established, it defeats both modal bridges about the same
A and G, whichever side supports it; it does not defeat the modal
consequence arguments, categorical arguments, bridges about another
consequence or arguments in other contexts; it does not suspend the
decision on A; a tie goes against the critic. A source-based scheme that
asserts the exception (expert opinion, position to know) stays an answer
too, each with its own questions (D095 (3)). A reply that only says G could
be found out is still coded as arguing the exception without making it out
(guidelines OC15), with Other; the game refuses it at that question from
step C5.2 (D095 (3)). The precautionary allocation, which puts the burden
on the side that advocates the action when harm may be severe or
irreversible (Walton 2014, p. 214), is not adopted: the bridges already let
a possible harm count against an action, the declaration's allocation
stands, and whether precaution is a bridge of its own stays candidate
C008's open question.

### 1. A trial before the four-day week

**as an answer** in the game: walton/worth_finding_out_first of the four-day week, bridged (proposal D008-D074's example), the bridge from a possible good; it concludes that bridge's exception, worth_finding_out_first(A,G); our own text.

> A three-month trial in one team would show cheaply, and in time, whether productivity rises before the whole firm commits.

Bindings: A = “the firm switching to a four-day week”; G = “higher productivity at the firm”; I = “a three-month trial in one team”.

- “A three-month trial in one team” would settle, reliably enough for the decision, whether action “the firm switching to a four-day week” brings about “higher productivity at the firm”.
- “A three-month trial in one team” can be done before the decision on action “the firm switching to a four-day week” must be taken.
- The costs and risks of “a three-month trial in one team” are worth bearing to know whether action “the firm switching to a four-day week” brings about “higher productivity at the firm” before that possibility counts in the decision.
- **So:** It is worth finding out first whether action “the firm switching to a four-day week” would bring about “higher productivity at the firm”, and until then that possibility should not count in deciding on the action: it can be found out in time, reliably enough to settle the question, at a cost and risk worth bearing.

Pressed with its questions:

- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise ““A three-month trial in one team” can be done before the decision on action “the firm switching to a four-day week” must be taken”: The board votes on the new contracts in two weeks; a three-month trial would report after the decision.
- **Challenge** (CQ:premise_acceptable), at the premise ““A three-month trial in one team” would settle, reliably enough for the decision, whether action “the firm switching to a four-day week” brings about “higher productivity at the firm””: One team of five, in its busiest quarter, would tell us little about the firm's three hundred people.

*Note:* The text names the investigation (the trial), says it is timely and cheap, and what it would settle: all three premises are made out, two of them explicitly. A critic of the answer challenges one of them. The bridge's defender need not show again that the possibility matters: matters_to_decision is the bridge's premise, not the answer's. Established, the exception leaves the possible gain out of the decision until the trial reports; the firm may still decide, on its other arguments.

### 5. No study has found harm

**a near miss** of `finding_out_first`: it is `ignorance`; our own text.

> No study has found that the pesticide harms bees, and if it did we would know by now, so it does not.

Bindings: S = “the pesticide harms bee colonies”.

- “The pesticide harms bee colonies” would be known if it were true.
- It is not the case that “the pesticide harms bee colonies” is known to be true.
- *Unless:* The truth of “the pesticide harms bee colonies” has not been sufficiently investigated.
- **So:** It is not the case that the pesticide harms bee colonies.

*Note:* An argument from ignorance: it concludes that the link is false from the lack of evidence, where finding_out_first concludes that the link should be settled before its possibility counts. Its own exception, uninvestigated(S), asks whether it was investigated enough; a reply that a study would settle it in time argues that exception, for another conclusion than this scheme's. It is the defensive side's claim that further investigation is not needed (Walton 1996, p. 122).

### 6. Until we know, no licence

**a near miss** of `finding_out_first`: it is `bridge_from_possible_harm`; our own text.

> The pesticide might harm bees, and harm to pollinators matters whatever the odds, so it should not be licensed until we know.

Bindings: A = “the agency licensing the pesticide”; G = “harm to bee colonies”.

- Action “the agency licensing the pesticide” may be unwise to perform because of “harm to bee colonies”.
- The chance that action “the agency licensing the pesticide” brings about “harm to bee colonies” is large enough, given what that consequence would be, to bear on whether the action should be performed.
- *Unless:* It is worth finding out first whether action “the agency licensing the pesticide” would bring about “harm to bee colonies”, and until then that possibility should not count in deciding on the action: it can be found out in time, reliably enough to settle the question, at a cost and risk worth bearing.
- **So:** It is not the case that action “the agency licensing the pesticide” should be performed.

*Note:* Precaution, not finding out first: it lets the possibility decide against the action now (the bridge from a possible harm, with its premise that the chance matters, as today's rules code a precautionary claim; whether precaution is a bridge of its own is candidate C008's third open question). 'Until we know' does not argue the bridges' exception: it names no investigation, and it asks for the possibility to count, not to be left out until settled.
