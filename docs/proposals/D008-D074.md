## Candidates: sketched, not proposed

Three schemes are sketched beside the proposal, never applied by it.

**The bridges** (the owner's choice 1) let a modal conclusion meet the
thesis, as D011 bridges popular opinion's reason_in_favor(A) to a claim
and D063 bridges conclusions that name an agent. Each is an explicit
warrant with its own challenge: a possibility counts for (or against)
doing the action unless it is too remote to act on. The bridge is never
counted as evidence beside the modal argument it bridges; it is the only
way the modal argument reaches should_be_performed(A). They would enter
walton.yml with the game, at step C5.

**The appeal to consequences** (C011, D074) concludes that a consequence
argument offered under a claim of what is the case is undercut as support
for that claim: undercut(X), X naming the argument, as D056's
parallel_argument_refutation does. In the game, rule S9 already refuses a
consequence scheme at a factual claim; the objection is for coding the
Kialo replies that raise it by name. Its exception is the case where the
claim at issue is itself whether to believe something, which consequences
can bear on (a pragmatic argument for belief, as in Pascal's wager).

### 1. A tax on meat would combat unhealthy meat consumption

**as an argument**, with `positive_consequences`; Kialo 13292.115.

> A tax on meat would combat unhealthy meat consumption.

Bindings: A = “governments putting a tax on meat”; G = “less unhealthy meat consumption”.

- The action “governments putting a tax on meat” brings about “less unhealthy meat consumption”.
- “Less unhealthy meat consumption” is good.
- *Unless:* The action “governments putting a tax on meat” has bad consequences that outweigh “less unhealthy meat consumption”.
- **So:** Action “governments putting a tax on meat” should be performed.

Pressed with its questions:

- **33/CQ1**, at the premise “The action “governments putting a tax on meat” brings about “less unhealthy meat consumption””: Meat eaters in the countries that tried it bought cheaper cuts rather than less meat.
- **game/CQ:severity**, at the premise ““Less unhealthy meat consumption” is good”: Most people already eat less meat than the guidelines' limit; the excess is a small group's.
- **game/CQ:bearer**, at the premise ““Less unhealthy meat consumption” is good”: The health gain would go to the heaviest eaters, who can most afford the tax and change least.

*Note:* Categorical: 'would combat'. The gold's coding; its G is phrased as the good state the tax brings about.

### 4. A sin tax on meat would make food more expensive

**as an argument**, with `negative_consequences`; Kialo 13292.7.

Pressed with its questions:

- **game/CQ:severity**, at the premise ““Food becoming more expensive for people who cannot afford it” is bad”: The tax adds a few cents to a meal; it is a nuisance, not hardship.

- **33/CQ3**, at the exception “The action “governments putting a tax on meat” has good consequences that outweigh “food becoming more expensive for people who cannot afford it””: The health and climate gains of the tax outweigh a small rise in food prices (good_outweighs_bad argues it).
