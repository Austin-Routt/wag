Generated by `wag-cli --proposal` from [`D008-D074.yml`](D008-D074.yml); edit that file, not this one. The decisions are in [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md); how proposals work is in [`process.md`](../process.md) §5.

**What changes.**
- **D008.** Two schemes of their own, modal variants of 33.1 and 33.2, as
  cause to effect has cause_to_effect_modal (D039):
  positive_consequences_modal and negative_consequences_modal. Their causal
  premise is modal, may_bring_about(A,G) ("the action A may bring about
  G"), so a possible consequence cannot fill the categorical
  brings_about(A,G) of 33.1 and 33.2. Their conclusions keep the modality
  and name the consequence: may_be_worth_performing(A,G) and
  may_be_unwise_to_perform(A,G). That is WAG's design: the book concludes
  'A should be brought about' and carries the strength in its questions,
  whose first asks whether the consequences will, may or must occur
  (Walton, Reed and Macagno 2008, pp. 102, 332-333). So the modal variants
  are linked, partly, to 33.1 and 33.2 (proposed below), and keep the
  book's three questions: likelihood and evidence at may_bring_about(A,G),
  whether the possibility is real rather than merely conceivable, and the
  sided outweighing at their exception (D055). They share the categorical
  schemes' sided exceptions: a claim that other consequences of A matter
  more than G compares magnitudes, which do not depend on how likely G is,
  and defeats a categorical and a modal argument about the same A and G
  alike; a weighing that relies on G's being only possible is a reply to
  the bridge's premise (below). 33.3 gets no modal variant: D008 names 33.1
  and 33.2; a hedged consequence for the agent's own goals is coded with
  negative_consequences_modal, 33.3 named in notes.
- **D008's bridges.** A modal conclusion meets the thesis,
  should_be_performed(A) or its negation, only through a bridge, one for
  each side, on D011's and D063's pattern (an explicit warrant, never
  counted as evidence beside the reason it bridges):
  bridge_from_possible_good and bridge_from_possible_harm. Each has two
  premises: the modal conclusion, and matters_to_decision(A,G), that the
  chance that A brings about G is large enough, given what G would be, to
  bear on whether A should be performed, which its arguer must support (an
  exception alone would be presumed not to hold, D063's refinement): a
  remote chance of a modest G does not, a small chance of a grave one may.
  Each has an exception of its own, knowable_before_deciding(A,G): whether
  A would bring about G can be found out before A must be decided (a
  trial, a pilot, a study), which an answer names. The bridge is an entry
  condition, not a weight: no multiplier is set, and the tests declare
  their weights equal, so that a bridged possible harm against a certain
  good of the same weight is a tie the weights or the outweighing
  exceptions decide, as for any two arguments. The exception was chosen
  over double counting (another argument already counts G), which has no
  effect in WAG's engine (it does not add arguments' weights, candidate
  C019) and so would not defeat anything. The bridges enter walton.yml now
  and the game at step C5.
- **D065.** Exceptions on positive and negative consequences and their
  modal variants, kept apart from good(G) and bad(G), so that a real but
  slight consequence is not a denied one: too_slight(A,G), that G is too
  slight a consequence to count in deciding on A (C005); and, for on whom
  it falls (C021), a sided pair: gain_discounted_by_bearer(A,G) on the
  schemes for A, that the gain G counts for less because of whom it would
  go to, and harm_discounted_by_bearer(A,G) on the schemes against A, that
  the harm G counts for less because of whom it would fall on. Who bears a
  consequence is not neutral between the sides: that a cost falls on the
  polluters who cause the harm discounts it as a harm and is the very
  reason it is a gain, so one formula on both sides would let a reason for
  Pro defeat Pro's own argument (D055's fault, which the engine showed in
  the review of version 2). They are exceptions, the critic's to support
  (D003): who must answer a question once it is asked is a rule of the
  dialogue (D075, item F), not an assumption. There is no threshold of how
  many a consequence reaches: one grave consequence can decide. They are
  asked as walton/ questions; the game asks them from step C5. 33.3 and
  33.4 do not get them: C005's and C021's recorded outcomes name 33.1 and
  33.2, whose forms all their sightings answer; 33.3 and 33.4 argue from
  the agent's own goals or welfare, so on whom the consequence falls is
  fixed by their form, and whether they need a seriousness exception waits
  for a sighting (recorded on C005).
- **D066.** No formula changes. Mitigation, the action done so that the
  harm does not follow (C006), is an amended action: a claim that argues
  for it argues for that action, coded with its own scheme and the role
  action_amendment (guidelines §2). Another action that brings the same
  good at less cost (C017) is a separate alternative, coded as OC6 has
  it: against practical reasoning it answers the better-means exception
  (better_means, C017's recorded outcome); against a consequence argument,
  which has no such exception, a claim that the other course is better is
  coded with other, naming C017, and one that only puts it forward is a
  proposal of another course (action_amendment, move alternative_means).
  That the action cannot be carried out at all (C018) attacks the causal
  premise brings_about(A,G): an answer to its likelihood question
  (33/CQ1; in the game and the gold, CQ:likelihood), since an action that
  cannot be done brings nothing about.
- **D067.** No change: a right, a duty or a value set against

- **D069.** The prudential form (33.4) gains the exception
  fails_to_avert(X,C): doing X would fail to avert C (C016), the critic's
  to support, as the owner decided (D003). Fear (36) states the converse
  as a premise (only_way_prevent). A harm of not acting that falls on
  others stays a coding rule, positive consequences of the action with the
  harm's not happening as G (OC13), not a scheme. Such a coding records
  whether the action's preventing the harm is explicit, implicit, unknown
  or disputed, within the record format the owner decided (D092): explicit
  is the causal premise's status explicit; implicit, status inferred, or
  supplied with a note that begins "implicit:"; unknown, status supplied
  with a note that begins "unknown:"; disputed, a reply in the discussion
  against that premise (a claim that targets it, polarity against). A
  challenge record is not one: it lists the questions an instance is open
  to, which every consequence instance is. The question exists; that does
  not make the link a fact.
- **D072.** A scheme of its own, harm_reduction (C022). Its premises are
  scoped to the action: E happens whether or not A is done
  (happens_anyway(A,E)), and doing A makes each occurrence of E less
  harmful (less_harmful_per_occurrence(A,E)); so A should be performed.
  Its exceptions are the owner's: a course open to whoever would do A
  could prevent E (preventable_otherwise(A,E); an answer names the
  course), and the harm E does overall would not fall
  (no_net_reduction(A,E)): a more frequent E can offset the gain per
  occurrence, though it need not (15 occurrences at 20 is less than 10 at
  100). A's other harms are argued as its negative consequences, a
  rebuttal at its conclusion; the scheme has no outweighing exception, so
  a claim that those harms matter more than the reduction is that
  rebuttal, with the comparison named in notes. C022's steering form ('it
  is coming anyway, so shape it') is not covered.
- **D074.** The appeal to consequences offered for the truth of a claim
  (C011) becomes an exception of the consequence schemes, 33.1 to 33.4 and
  the modal variants: offered_for_truth(A), that the consequences cited
  about A are offered as a reason to think a claim true, not as a reason
  about whether to do A. The decision allows a question or an undercutter;
  the exception is the question's form in walton.yml (D003), needs no new
  vocabulary for an argument's identity, and answers the Kialo replies that
  raise the objection by name against a consequence argument coded by OC1
  (its practical conclusion meeting nothing at a factual target). Its
  reach: once argued, it undercuts every consequence argument about A in
  the case, of either side; in the case it is for, those arguments meet
  nothing at the factual target already, so no label changes: what it adds
  is the record of the objection. A claim that concludes what is the case
  from consequences is coded with other (OC1); a reply that raises the
  objection against it undercuts that argument's inference. In a
  discussion of whether to believe something, the exception does not hold:
  there the consequences of believing bear on the question.

**The polarity pairs (D053).** 33.1 and 33.2 are a declared pair (D055).
Their new formulas are shared or flipped: too_slight(A,G) and
offered_for_truth(A) are shared and declared neutral as to polarity (if G
is too slight to count, it counts for neither side; if the consequences of
A are offered for the truth of a claim, no consequence argument about A
bears on it), and the bearer exceptions are a sided flip
(gain_discounted_by_bearer and harm_discounted_by_bearer, with the same
arguments). The bridges are a pair too, declared in families.json: their
conclusions are p and ¬p, their first premises a sided flip
(may_be_worth_performing and may_be_unwise_to_perform), matters_to_decision
a shared premise, and knowable_before_deciding a shared exception, declared
neutral (if it can be found out whether A brings about G, neither side
should act on the possibility). Defeating one side's bridge leaves the
other's standing when they cite different consequences; when they cite
the same G, valued oppositely, finding out settles both, as declared. The
modal variants conclude two predicates, a sided flip, not p and ¬p, so
they are variants, as C003's answer schemes are; their shared exceptions
are the same neutral ones. D053 asks a person to declare each neutral
formula: the proposal proposes the declarations, with the reasons above,
and the engine tests each with one example per side, each side proving it
for a reason of its own (effects); the owner makes them (below). The
standing rule replaces the owner's reading of a proposal, not what the
register gives to a person (process.md).

### 1. A tax on meat would combat unhealthy meat consumption

**as an argument**, with `positive_consequences`; Kialo 13292.115.

> A tax on meat would combat unhealthy meat consumption.

Bindings: A = “governments putting a tax on meat”; G = “less unhealthy meat consumption”.

- The action “governments putting a tax on meat” brings about “less unhealthy meat consumption”.
- “Less unhealthy meat consumption” is good.
- *Unless:* The action “governments putting a tax on meat” has bad consequences that outweigh “less unhealthy meat consumption”.
- *Unless:* In deciding on action “governments putting a tax on meat”, “less unhealthy meat consumption” is too slight a consequence to count.
- *Unless:* In deciding on action “governments putting a tax on meat”, the gain “less unhealthy meat consumption” counts for less because of whom it would go to.
- *Unless:* The consequences cited about “governments putting a tax on meat” are offered as a reason to think a claim true, not as a reason about whether to do it.
- **So:** Action “governments putting a tax on meat” should be performed.

Pressed with its questions:

- **33/CQ1**, at the premise “The action “governments putting a tax on meat” brings about “less unhealthy meat consumption””: Meat eaters in the countries that tried it bought cheaper cuts rather than less meat.
- **walton/too_slight**, at the exception “In deciding on action “governments putting a tax on meat”, “less unhealthy meat consumption” is too slight a consequence to count”: Most people already eat less meat than the guidelines' limit; the gain would be a small change in a few diets.
- **walton/gain_discounted_by_bearer**, at the exception “In deciding on action “governments putting a tax on meat”, the gain “less unhealthy meat consumption” counts for less because of whom it would go to”: The gain would go to the heaviest eaters, who chose their diet and could change it without a tax.

*Note:* Categorical: 'would combat' (the mood rule). The gold's coding.
