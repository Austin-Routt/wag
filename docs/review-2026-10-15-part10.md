## 2. Is there a definitive matrix?

**No single one, and not in the book.** *The New Dialectic* lists the six main types and their subtypes (p. 31) and describes each in chapters 1–7. It has no table of them; its only figures are of other things. The tables in circulation come from Walton's other works, as Aberdein's survey reproduces them:

| Table | What it does | Source | In the reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The grid** | **Derives** the six main types from two factors: the initial situation (a conflict; an open problem; an unsatisfactory spread of information) and the main goal (a stable agreement; a practical settlement; a provisional accommodation). Six cells are filled, three are empty. | Walton & Krabbe 1995, p. 80 | [§2.2](dialogue-types.md#22-why-six-the-grid) |
| **The descriptive table** | **Describes** each type: initial situation, each party's goal, the collective goal, the benefit. Its rows vary: eight in 1997, with debate and pedagogical dialogue added. | Walton 1997 (and earlier versions in 1990 and 1995) | [§2.3](dialogue-types.md#23-the-descriptive-table-walton-1997-via-a20-table-2) |

**Which one WAG uses:**
- **The grid** is the closest thing to a definitive matrix, because it explains *why* there are six main types.
- **WAG's working definition** is the six main types of the book, placed by the grid and described from the book itself ([reference §2.6, §4](dialogue-types.md#26-is-there-a-definitive-matrix-and-is-the-list-closed)).
- **Later lists by Walton and others** vary again, for example by adding a "discovery" dialogue. We have not reviewed them.

## 3. Advocacy and action

**Were they covered?**
- **Action-producing dialogue:** one line in the reference. Now it has a full row in [§2.4](dialogue-types.md#24-other-labels-and-how-nd-treats-them).
- **Advocacy:** only as the bias ad hominem and "advocacy advertising". That was a real gap, now filled in §2.4 and [§2.5](dialogue-types.md#25-advocacy-and-action-what-each-main-type-is-for).

**What the book says about advocacy** (pp. 121–5, 209, 215–7, 227–32):
- **Advocacy is normal in persuasion.** Each party argues its own view as strongly as the rules allow, so each side has a bias. That bias is acceptable unless it is pushed so far that it destroys the openness and balance the dialogue needs (p. 122).
- **A different kind of advocacy is interest-based:** arguing for a stake one has, which is bargaining. Doing so covertly while claiming to discuss is what the bias ad hominem alleges (pp. 121–2).
- **A sales pitch is "some kind of advocacy dialogue"** (p. 217). Analysed closely, it is a mixture: information-seeking, then persuasion, then negotiation, with the buyers possibly deliberating (pp. 227–32).
- **Advertisements split two ways.** A commercial advertisement is like an offer in a negotiation; an advocacy advertisement is an argument in a persuasion dialogue (pp. 123–5).
- **Advocacy limits one's duties.** An advocate need not present the other side's evidence. A consulted expert, or an inquiry, owes completeness (pp. 230–2).
- **The book leaves advocacy open.** It says bias and advocacy remain to be investigated (p. 122).

**What the book says about action-producing dialogue** (pp. 199–200, 223):
- **The phrase is not defined in the book.** "Action-producing" is one of five models that Walton found mixed in the Canadian Question Period (after Walton 1992), because one of its stated aims is to press the government to act.
- **"Action-directed" names deliberation in practice.** The bicycle-path exchange about which road to take is called action-directed, then a deliberation. Question Period is said to be, in part, an action-directed deliberation.

**Do the main types advocate, or produce action?** The key passage is p. 108:
- **In negotiation and deliberation, commitment is to a course of action.**
- **In persuasion, commitment is an opinion:** accepting a proposition as true.

So, type by type:

| Type | Advocates? | Produces action? |
|---|---|---|
| Persuasion | **Yes,** by design | **No:** acceptance of a thesis. But the thesis may be that an action is advisable (p. 229), as in Kialo's policy debates. |
| Negotiation | **Yes,** for interests | **Yes:** a deal is a set of pledges |
| Deliberation | **Partly:** proposals, with a shared goal | **Yes:** a decision to act, held tentatively |
| Inquiry | **No** | **No:** knowledge |
| Information-seeking | **Not the answerer** | **Indirectly:** it serves the questioner's task |
| Eristic | **Extremely** | **No:** catharsis |

**What this means for the Kialo gold: nothing changes in the coding of claims.**
- **The setting:** a Kialo discussion is advocacy within a persuasion dialogue, usually about a practical thesis.
- **The type:** guidelines step 0 records it as persuasion with a practical thesis.
- **The other side's evidence:** the sides are not expected to present it, so leaving it out is not a fault in itself.
- **For other corpora,** the reference's data model has an optional per-party `advocates` field: a point of view, an interest, or none.
